    About

    In a park where nobody holds the whole map, the layout is the information. The visitors and maintenance robots here only know what they have seen, so where the signposts stand, which lawns are open and how the money is split decide both whether visitors find what they came for and whether the robots can keep the park looking good. Those goals pull against each other, so we don't try to guess the best layout. We search for it by running the park.

    This is the Lake and the Ramble in Central Park, rebuilt from NYC Open Data. Visitors come in at the entrances, look for a few places before their time runs out, and find their way back. Mowers, stewards and skimmers work out of a charging center at Cherry Hill.

    Environment designer

    You are the designer. An environment is which of the 32 signposts stand (green dots), which lawns are closed (red), and the total budget: signposts cost $2k each and the rest buys robots. Each environment is scored by running it: half for visitors (places seen, on time), half for the park (time presentable), minus 0.04 points per $1k spent. Red shading on the maps is where agents walked.

    or start over from
    Each epoch makes two variations of the current environment, runs them on the same visitors and weather, and keeps whichever of the three scores best.
